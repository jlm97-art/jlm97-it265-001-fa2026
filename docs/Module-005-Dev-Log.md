#### Learnings
- A game mechanic is a rule-governed action/process that may change throughout the session of a game
  - Game mechanics may be built on actions (something the player or game does), rules (constraints and consequences set by the game), and goals (what the player is trying to achieve by these elements).
  - Such actions may be influenced by elements in a way where the player may face difficulty trying to adjust plan their actions, adapt to the rules, or achieve the goal. These elements may be the current state (such as who's turn it is in a board game), space (position), and feedback (response to a player's actions, may be positive or negative)
- Games that require observation may include puzzles, which arise from the understanding of relationships and rules established by the game
  - Such puzzles may include pattern recognition, space management, or logic
  - Puzzles must be visually and/or logically fair - players need to be able to make observations that influence the approaches of their next attempt
  - An action taken by the player, or a choice that they make, needs to have some type of consequence.
    - This does not have to solely be a restriction or negative all the time, rather a game needs to introduce a sort
      of reward to make the decision outweigh the potential risks or challenges the player may face.
- Strategy and tactics in a game do not hold the same definition.
  - Strategy is pivoted towards a set of moves that help achieve a longer-term goal, such as a session-wide goal or spanning
    over many turns (For example, saving up a large amount of money in a board game to purchase a game-winning item)
  - Tactics are a set of actions that focus more on achieving a shorter-term goal, whether it be for the next turn or next
    few turns (Such as conserving resources - health for example - to make it to the next turn in an RPG)
