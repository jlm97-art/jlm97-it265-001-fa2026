# IT265 Module 2: One-Page Treatment

**Working game title: Freaky Taxi** 

1. **Premise:** In "Freaky Taxi", the players control figurines representing each player. Said players must navigate a board using the default dice rolls, board event spaces, and item dice sets in order to not only reach the end of the board, but also meet a certain "currency" quota increased by board event spaces to unlock the exit gate before other players.
2. **Experience and loop:** Each playthrough of *Freaky Taxi* should feel distinct from the previous one, where major aspects of the game - from dice rolls to item collection to strategy based on board layout - should feel strategic instead of repetitive in terms of approach.
3. **Goal and pressure:** Players' quota and inventory change as the rounds progress. Players might have more items during one round and less during the next, along with their currency quota. Two major restrictions are placed on each player, those being an inventory limit to prevent hoarding dice items, and the amount of turns each player has, which is always at one. The game ends when one player has reached the exit and has met the specified currencyquota along the way.
4. **Scope:** This is the first version of my board game, which will be distinctive from later versions as it cuts out a core element for testing purposes: key items. Such items will be optional but beneficial to players who collect them. In terms of a physical prototype, the most accessible materials that the game can be built out of are cardboard boards for the map layout, and makeshift figurines representing each player. In addition to this, tokens can also be used for the materialistic currency quota, and existing dice sets can also be used to represent a player's movement per round.

## Treatment Draft

In *Freaky Taxi*, players must navigate their figurine to the exit gate of the board and meet the specific "currency" quota while doing so. Turn order is decided on a dice roll at the start of the game, in which the lowest dice roll value moves first. Movement and quota collection throughout the board are influenced not just by the default dice set, but also by items that players might find on board spaces or event board spaces themselves. 
Freaky Taxi is meant to match the feel of a randomizer, or a roguelike game. No two playthroughs should feel exactly the same, and players start off by making their initial moves on the board whilst potentially collecting helpful items, slowly amassing a bigger item and quota pool to ensure victory by the time they reach the exit gate. Although the list of items and the board layout are constant variables, such majors aspects of the game should make each playthrough have the player come up with a strategic approach. This may be based on their turn order, quota, place on the board, and their item inventory.
Throughout the game, players might find their quota or item inventory changing drastically. One round, they might have a stockpile of item dice to help with movement or quota collection and the next round such resources could be spent or taken away by another player, all while having to manage with the idea of a limited inventory space and moving only once per round. The game ends once a player has reached the exit gate first while meeting the given currency quota. Although several key features are planned to be implemented in the final version, this first version only includes basic aspects of the game. Implementing only the quota, the default dice, and a few item dice sets,
this version will be distinct from other versions of the board game in order to gauge how long a casual playthrough might take. Though *Freaky Taxi* is a game meant for casual audiences, it is primarily designed strategy and item/turn-based genres, and therefore also for strategists and fast-paced players.
