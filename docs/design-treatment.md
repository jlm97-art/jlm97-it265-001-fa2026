**FREAKY TAXI**

Game Design Treatment

**Designer:** [Jon Tyler Munar]

**Updated:** [10/19/2026]



## Game Concept

[I decided on the name "Freaky Taxi" for this strategic board game as it aims to capture the chaotic day-to-day experience
of trying to get around large cities (New York as a prominent example). The title is a first impression that the board game
will try to replicate the feeling of trying to haul a cab, scrounging enough money for a bus fare, or simply just trying to
get to the next train on time through board and dice.]

[In Freaky Taxi, players compete against one another (or in teams) to collect the required amount of currency coins in order to
access the exit gate - or in the case of this game's setting, the taxi - before the other players (or other team) manage to do so. In story terms, players must acquire
enough money to pay for the last taxi home. The themes of this board game are wealth acquisition, exploration, and strategy,]

## Gameplay


[Goals: Acquiring enough tokens to unlock the exit gate first, accumulating a set of item dice to help the player with collecting currencies, moving spaces,
and sabotaging other players
Ending: When a player first reaches the exit taxi before all other players. 
In team-based gameplay, the player from one team that reaches the end first wins the game for that team.
Challenges: Maintaining a sizeable amount of currency coins and item dice sets, adapting to player sabotage strategies and random chance events from tiles, keeping close to the exit gate,
strategizing for when other players may be at a noticeable advantage]
 
[Explain the core loop: what the player considers, chooses or does, and how that changes the next decision.
The player considers their current inventory of items, what space they are on the map layout, and where other players are in order to strategize based on what
they currently have, what other players have, and where they currently are. The player also also may choose to land on a certain spot, or use a specific dice item
to mitigate any negative effects imposed by other players on the next coming turns.]
### Setup And Start


[A game is first set up by deciding turn order. Each player rolls a D-20, and turn order is decided based on ascending dice value. The person who rolls the
highest dice value goes first. A spinner is then used to place the exit spots on the map, from 1 to 3, for each of the 3 islands. The spinner is then used again to determine which island all players will start at.]

### Core Gameplay Loop

[Throughout the game, players would ideally be focused on the current position on one of the three islands, and the types of tiles ahead of their position in order to plan for the next turn (I.E. choosing what roll value would be optimal, or what item dice sets they should use as tactics for the next few turns). After rolling the dice and landing on a certain value, the player moves that many spaces forward, and depending on what kind of space they land on, they may gain currency coins (flat addition or multiplied), or lose currency coins (flat subtraction or divided). In addition to this, landing on a certain space may trigger unique events, such as all players gaining/losing a certain amount of coins, or gaining the opportunity to buy item dice.]

### Ending And Outcome

[A session ends when a player stands on the exit tile with the specified amount of currency coins. For a free-for-all-session, the win condition would be if you are the first player to meet the currency quota whilst standing on the tile. For team-based sessions, the first player who meets said conditions will count as a win for that player's team.]

## First Physical Prototype


[The planned prototype will consist of three triangular islands, each being connected with straight and curved roads. The board's material will consist of poster paper for the surface (with the tile layout of each island being traced and outlined), and cardboard underneath for a sturdy structure for long play sessions. The currency coins will mainly take the
form of gold-colored tokens. The dice items will consist of different-colored dice sets, and will include D6 and D20 dice sets. The spinner used for certain events will also be made of a cardboard and poster-paper material, and cards used for certain events will make use of index cards. For the figurines, each figurine will represent a different player, in which these figurines will also be made of cardboard.]


[The board is the main physical play area, accessible to all players. Each island represents a third of the board, and depending on the player's current position, one island may be more accessible to some players than others. ]

### Materials And Mechanic Adjustments

[Cardboard: A material used for almost every asset of the board game, utilized for its sturdiness compare to regular paper
 Metal tokens: Used as the currency, chosen as the currency type in order to replicate the feeling of actual monetary value while holding no actual value outside the board game (like poker chips not holding any value outside a casino)
 RULES:
      - Turn order is decided on a D20 dice, first to last is dependent on who roles the higher value
      - Players can only move on their turn, or by a random event tile condition (such as swapping positions with another player). On their turn, they can take one of two actions: Move with the default dice, use an item dice to help with movement or to help with currency coin collection
        -If in the event the player uses an item dice, they can use no more than one item dice along with the default item dice (No more than 2 dice in play per player essentially). The default item dice can be swapped out for another item dice should the player have more than one item dice.
      - Players can hold a maximum of 3 item dice at the beginning of the game, which can be increased by landing on rare backpack spaces that increase their maximum inventory slot by 1 for every time they land on it.
      - Players can hold a max of X currency coins at a time to ensure that a player can't "hoard" every coin that comes with a copy of the board game. Should a player be in a situation where they earn an amount of coins that causes their total to go over the limit, the player will only receive the amount of coins that adds up to the limit.
      - In the event the player holds a yellow dice (an item dice that grants more coins), they can opt to roll for more tokens, though no more than 1 can be used per turn.
      - When entering an item shop, players can buy a maximum of 4 items. Constant limit throughout the session.]

## Intended Players


[Intended for both casual players, strategists, sightseers, item-gatherers
Casual players: A primary intended audience as the simple decisions of rolling a dice, choosing to use items, and basic item variants / event spaces / currency system are meant to serve as a good introduction to strategy board games
Strategists: Event spaces and item dice variants are abundant across the board and game's intended items, allowing for a variety of approaches given the current available resources one player may have.
Sightseers: An audience influenced by the city theme of this board game. Traveling to each island on the board aims to capture the experience of moving around a city.
Item-gatherers: Variety of items to help the user or sabotage other users, in addition to a simple item-collection system helps appeal to this audience as it promotes stockpiling items for future strategies]

## Accessibility And Inclusivity


[Color palette choice: This board game utilizes a complementary color palette to help differentiate between harmful and helpful effects. Alongside this, such color palette also uses a light and dark brightness contrast to help support this distinction. This promotes accessibility as players may have certain conditions such as color blindness, in which a generic color palette might confuse them.
Visual design for tiles: The icons of certain tiles on the board also are meant to clearly distinguish between what may be a helpful or harmful effect, in which the only exception to this would be the event tile (marked with an "!"). This is to also help players who might have certain difficulties telling such tiles apart, similar to the color blindness issue for the color palette.
Simplistic objective rule set: The generic ruleset (that focuses on the overall goal) of the game is kept to a relatively short list in order to be understandable to players of all skill levels, but to promote inclusivity as this helps introduce players that are unfamiliar with strategy and dice-focused board games into these genres.
Simplistic item and currency system rule set: Effects of items and how currency is traded around is kept to a simplified degree in order to not overwhelm the players with having to keep track of too much available resources and potentially upcoming (dis)advantages. Item pool is relatively small to help players understand the significance of these objects during play without overwhelming them with too many choices to approach their current situation. By keeping a relatively small list of items and rules for earning and losing currency, the conditions and resources of the board game still grants players ample agency for a variety of strategies.
Tile mechanics:  Tile mechanics are straightforward in what they do to prevent confusion or overwhelm as to what effects may befall onto the player while still keeping play interesting. Almost all tiles are labeled in a way where there effect is obvious, with the exception of the event tile which may grant one of several benefits or disadvantages across one or multiple players.]

## Unique Selling Points (USP)


[Explain the game's unique selling points and the relevant comparison.
Unique item pool: Different types of item dice are accessible to the player throughout sessions, whether bought, earned, or swapped between players. Each has the potential to help the player gain an advantage, or gain even ground with another player in terms of currency or position on the board.
Fair balance between randomness and control: The inclusion of items grants a certain agency to players in terms of what regions they want to move to on the board, how much currency to earn, and certain ways to avoid sabotage by other players. However, the game still relies on dice for movement, shuffling effect cards for event tiles, and a spinner for certain tiles in order to keep a sense of randomness.
Ever-changing strategy: During every turn players must consider what island they are on, what spaces are ahead and behind them, what items they currently possess, and how much currency coins they have.
Risk vs. reward: Players are able to take certain paths upon arriving to each island, in which said paths may be safer, yet less rewarding than others, while others may put them at a higher ground at the cost of a potential disadvantage.
Replay value: Randomized setup at the start of the game allows for different approaches to gain advantages, and random factors ensure that strategies and the appropriate time to put them into motion do not feel scripted.
]

## Concept Pitch
[Freaky Taxi is a city-themed, strategy and item-stockpiler based board game that puts players in a high-stakes race to meet the currency quota and to reach the taxi tile before their opponents. Across three triangular islands, players will be able to explore the city board's surroundings, gather items to strategize against or plan for the actions of other players, and adapt to a variety of event tiles that can potentially bring one from rags to riches, or the other way around. Featuring a simplistic objective, a verity of key items, and easy-to-understand tiles and event cards, Freaky Taxi ensures that all kind of audiences - whether new to the inventory management and strategy board game genres or experienced in adapting a strategy in given circumstances - are able to easily understand and quickly pick up the core gameplay loop. Though luck is a key factor, Freaky Taxi offers a sense of agency with its set of advantageous items, a shop to trade coins for items, and certain decisions from event tiles so that a player can create a set of tactics rather than leaving their victory up to pure chance. ]
