#### Learnings
- Color theory is critical for the development of a game.
  - Colors heavily influence the theme of a game, and the type of mood a player might feel when exploring.
  - Colors are not only limited to just establishing a theme or mood, but it also contributes to the player's environment and immediate surroundings.
  - Color may be used to represent an item in range, or it may be used as a way to guide the player. The latter should be used more lightly, as too much indicators via color could lead to "yellow paint".
    - "Yellow Paint" is the concept of using too many visual indicators to guide the player instead of letting them explore their surroundings.
   
- A game should be able to make player action and player's surroundings feel as if the player is truly within the world of the game.
- This is accomplished by four aspects:
  - Ambience - involves building upon aspects of the human senses (sight, sound)
  - Storytelling - establishes context through mechanics, such as setting
  - Interface - Presents the status of a player, dialogue, and other mechanics that may need to be explained
  - Cohesion - set of "coordinated cues" that may build upon ambience
- All four of these aspects help build the idea of immersion. Too much of storytelling or interface can break such immersion as the player may not hold much input in the game.
